# **ORTHOPEDIC & SPINE SCENARIO MASTER SYSTEM**

**In-House Recommendations — Dr. William Tontz MD, CLCP**

This master document defines standardized clinical scenarios ("players") for spine and orthopedic conditions, organized from head to toe. Each scenario bundles Dr. Tontz's in-house recommendations for future medical care. These scenarios are designed as modular building blocks to be rotated into a life care plan depending on the patient's actual clinical pattern, treatment history, and documented benefit. 1

### **GLOBAL PRINCIPLES**

1. **Recommendations must be tied to treatments documented in the record and supported by benefit or clear clinical rationale.**  Dr. Tontz will have recommendations that are inhouse recommendations for the body parts below that will be tied to treatments or a clear clinical rationale as deemed by him.

Do not add speculative care based only on imaging or diagnosis. 

2. **Independent recommendations are limited to orthopedic and spine care.** For neurology, psychiatry, ENT, cardiology, and other non-orthopedic/non-spine specialties, future care should be obtained from the treating providers or specialty experts. 3

3. **For any given body region, generic sprain or strain is not used as the primary diagnosis when a more specific structural diagnosis is present and causally related.** The structural diagnosis becomes the anchor for that region's scenario. 4

4. **STRUCTURAL INJURY SURVEILLANCE RULE:** Any structural injury to a body part (herniated disc, rotator cuff tear, labral tear, fracture, stenosis, cartilage damage, facet syndrome, radiculopathy, etc.) requires surveillance at a minimum of **radiographs and MRIs every other year for life expectancy.** Spine fusions additionally require a **CT scan every five years for life expectancy.** Sprains and strains do NOT require surveillance because they typically heal in 6-12 weeks and do not create long-term structural changes requiring monitoring.  
5. **Spine specialist follow-up** for cervical, thoracic, and lumbar regions is annually for life expectancy in both non-operative and post-fusion/disc replacement cases — but only when a structural diagnosis is present. If the spine body part is a sprain without any deeper structural diagnosis, then no follow-up items are needed. 5

6. **Pain management visit frequency** is based on the frequency of interventional procedures such as epidural steroid injections, medial branch blocks, and radiofrequency ablations. 6

7. **If there is cervical radiculopathy persisting within one year** of the life care plan and the patient has never had a cervical ESI, a one-time cervical ESI should be afforded. 7

8. **If the patient has beneficial medial branch or facet blocks and no prior RFA**, only a one-time RFA should be recommended. If there has been prior RFA with benefit, annual RFA may be recommended for life expectancy. 8

9. **Surgical recommendations** should be layered on top of non-surgical management, with a clear statement that surgery is indicated if appropriate non-surgical care fails. 9

## **I. SPINE SCENARIO SYSTEM**

### **A. CERVICAL SPINE SCENARIOS**

**Scenario C1 — Cervical Herniated Disc, Non-Operative**

* **Clinical pattern:** Structural cervical disc herniation and/or foraminal stenosis without prior cervical surgery, managed non-operatively. 10

* **Key record triggers:** Cervical MRI showing herniation and/or stenosis; no ACDF or disc replacement; no myelopathy; conservative care documented. 11

* **Default future recommendations:**  
  1. Spine specialist follow-up: annually for life expectancy. 12

  2. Imaging surveillance: **Cervical radiographs and cervical MRI every other year for life expectancy.**  
  3. Physical therapy: 24 visits every five years for life expectancy. 13

**Scenario C2 — Cervical Radiculopathy Within One Year of LCP, No Prior ESI**

* **Clinical pattern:** Active cervical radiculopathy that has persisted within one year of the life care plan, with no prior cervical epidural steroid injection. 14

* **Key record triggers:** Documented neurologic signs or symptoms of cervical radiculopathy in the last 12 months; MRI evidence of foraminal stenosis or herniation; no prior cervical ESI. 15

* **Default future recommendations:**  
  1. One-time cervical epidural steroid injection with professional and facility fees. 16

  2. One pain management visit associated with the cervical ESI. 17

  3. Spine specialist follow-up: annually for life expectancy. 18

  4. Imaging surveillance: **Cervical radiographs and cervical MRI every other year for life expectancy.** \[User instruction\]

**Scenario C3 — Cervical Radiculopathy With Prior Beneficial ESI**

* **Clinical pattern:** Cervical radiculopathy where prior cervical epidural steroid injections produced significant relief. 19

* **Key record triggers:** Cervical ESI procedure documentation; follow-up notes describing meaningful improvement. 20

* **Default future recommendations:**  
  1. Cervical ESI: up to three injections per year for life expectancy. 21

  2. Pain management visits: one visit per planned ESI. 22

  3. Spine specialist follow-up: annually for life expectancy. 23

  4. Imaging surveillance: **Cervical radiographs and cervical MRI every other year for life expectancy.**

**Scenario C4 — Cervical Facet Syndrome, Blocks Helped, No Prior RFA**

* **Clinical pattern:** Mechanical axial neck pain with documented benefit from diagnostic medial branch or facet blocks, and no prior RFA. 24

* **Key record triggers:** Facet or medial branch block procedures at cervical levels; post-procedure documentation of significant pain reduction. 25

* **Default future recommendations:**  
  1. One-time cervical radiofrequency ablation with professional and facility fees. 26

  2. One pain management visit associated with the cervical RFA. 27

  3. No recurring annual RFA unless later documented. 28

  4. Imaging surveillance: **Cervical radiographs and cervical MRI every other year for life expectancy.** \[User instruction\]

**Scenario C5 — Cervical Facet Syndrome With Prior Beneficial RFA**

* **Clinical pattern:** Cervical facet-mediated pain previously treated with RFA, with documented durable relief. 29

* **Key record triggers:** Prior cervical RFA procedure notes; follow-ups describing significant and sustained pain reduction. 30

* **Default future recommendations:**  
  1. Annual cervical RFA at the treated levels for life expectancy. 31

  2. Annual pain management visit. 32

  3. Spine specialist follow-up: annually for life expectancy. 33

  4. Imaging surveillance: **Cervical radiographs and cervical MRI every other year for life expectancy.** \[User instruction\]

**Scenario C6 — Post-Operative Cervical Fusion or Disc Replacement**

* **Clinical pattern:** Patient has undergone ACDF or cervical disc replacement causally related to the accident. 34

* **Key record triggers:** Operative report documenting ACDF or disc replacement; imaging showing instrumentation. 35

* **Default future recommendations:**  
  1. Spine specialist follow-up: annually for life expectancy. 36

  2. Imaging surveillance: **Cervical radiographs and cervical MRI every other year for life expectancy.**  
  3. CT Scan surveillance: **Cervical CT scan every 5 years for life expectancy.**  
  4. Post-operative physical therapy: 24 visits. 37

  5. Adjacent segment fusion anticipated at 17 years post-index surgery. 38
